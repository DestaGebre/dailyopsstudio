A promising idea is not the same as a validated opportunity. Validation asks a narrower question: **what must be true for this idea to deserve the next investment of time or money?**

The goal is not to prove that the idea is good. It is to reach a defensible decision quickly.

## Start with a Falsifiable Claim

Replace a broad statement such as “people need a better planning tool” with claims that evidence could contradict:

- A defined customer encounters the problem regularly.
- Existing alternatives leave an important job unfinished.
- The customer can recognize the value of a proposed solution.
- A reachable channel can put the offer in front of that customer.
- The likely price can support delivery, support and acquisition costs.

These are assumptions until evidence supports them.

## Test the Riskiest Assumption First

Do not begin with the easiest question. Begin with the condition most likely to stop the idea.

If a physical product cannot produce an acceptable contribution margin, calculate costs before studying logo concepts. If a service depends on access to a regulated profession, verify that access before interviewing a broad audience. If a digital product solves an occasional inconvenience, test whether buyers will make a meaningful commitment before building every feature.

The first test should be small enough to discard without regret.

## Collect Decision-Relevant Evidence

Useful early evidence may include:

- Direct descriptions of recent customer behavior
- Current prices and terms from accessible suppliers
- Credible competing offers and their positioning
- Search language that reveals how customers describe the problem
- A response to a concrete offer, prototype or pilot
- Regulatory or platform restrictions from primary sources

A search result, social-media comment or trend line can suggest a question. It rarely proves a market by itself. Record the source, date and limitation of each finding.

## Define the Gate Before Seeing Results

Write success and stop criteria in advance. For example:

- **Continue:** qualified prospects consistently describe the problem and some accept a meaningful next step.
- **Validate further:** the problem appears real, but price, channel or delivery economics remain uncertain.
- **Hold:** evidence is mixed and the next test is currently too expensive or inaccessible.
- **Reject:** a critical constraint fails or credible evidence contradicts the core assumption.

Predefined gates reduce the temptation to reinterpret weak signals after the test.

## End with One Next Action

Research is complete when it changes a decision. The output should state what the evidence supports, what remains assumed, why the current decision follows, and the smallest next action.

DailyOps users can preserve that chain in the [Market Research workspace](/dashboard/market-research), but the method works in any notebook or spreadsheet: claim, evidence, score, decision, next action.

**Practical takeaway:** validate the condition that could kill the idea, not the features that make it exciting.
