Kitchen cleaning is not one end-of-day event. The most reliable routines divide work into tasks completed before service, during service, after service, and during final verification.

This structure makes expectations easier to follow under pressure. It also helps supervisors spot the difference between a missed routine task and a maintenance issue that needs escalation.

## Structure the routine around the shift

- Before service: inspect surfaces, sinks, equipment, and supplies
- During service: control spills, waste, tools, and high-touch areas
- After service: clean equipment, floors, storage, and preparation areas
- Final verification: review completion and record exceptions

Use a consistent printable checklist near the kitchen workflow. Staff should initial their tasks, and the shift lead should verify the full area before close. The result is a clearer standard that does not depend on who happens to be working.
