Market research becomes useful when it changes what happens next. DailyOps is designed around that principle: an opportunity should move from a question to evidence, a gate, a decision and one bounded action.

The system does not treat a score as a forecast. It is a structured summary of the evidence currently available.

## What DailyOps Scores

Opportunity strength covers six questions:

- Is there evidence of demand?
- Is the customer problem meaningful?
- Does behavior suggest commercial intent?
- Is the proposed difference specific and relevant?
- Can the customer be reached?
- Can the product be delivered responsibly?

Risk scores cover startup cost, competition, operational complexity and legal or compliance exposure. High risk reduces the overall result rather than disappearing into a notes field.

## Evidence Comes Before a Decision

An opportunity can exist without evidence, but a DailyOps decision cannot. A newly created idea remains pending until at least one source-backed finding is recorded.

This is a minimum integrity rule, not a claim that one source is sufficient. The decision logic remains conservative when evidence is sparse. More evidence should not be collected merely to increase a count; it should address the assumptions capable of changing the decision.

## What the Four Decisions Mean

- **GO:** evidence and score support continuing, with no unresolved gate severe enough to stop progress.
- **VALIDATE:** the idea may be promising, but evidence, economics or blockers require a bounded test.
- **HOLD:** some evidence exists, but the current case is not strong enough to proceed or weak enough to reject.
- **REJECT:** weak opportunity strength or severe risk makes further investment unjustified under current assumptions.

A rejected idea is not a failed research project. Rejection can be the highest-value result when it prevents months of avoidable work.

## Every Decision Keeps Its Reason

DailyOps records the score, reason and next action with each decision. Later research does not erase the earlier record. This makes it possible to see what changed: a supplier quote invalidated the margin, a restriction introduced a blocker, or stronger customer behavior improved confidence.

The history is especially important when enthusiasm returns after the original evidence has been forgotten.

## The Next Action Must Be Measurable

“Do more research” is not a useful next action. A better instruction identifies the uncertainty, method and gate—for example, obtain three accessible supplier prices and reject the product if none supports the minimum contribution target.

The [DailyOps Market Research workspace](/dashboard/market-research) preserves this process privately for signed-in users. The framework itself is simple enough to use anywhere: separate facts from assumptions, score only what the evidence supports, and end with a decision.

**Practical takeaway:** the purpose of Market Lab is not to keep ideas alive. It is to make the next investment—or the decision to stop—explicit.
